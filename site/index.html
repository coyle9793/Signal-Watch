Insider trades

What members of Congress and company insiders (SEC Form 4) are buying and selling.

Where insiders agree

Stocks where several people with inside knowledge are trading the same way. Buying by several insiders at once is one of the best-studied signals. Selling is weaker evidence, since insiders often sell on a schedule for taxes or diversification.

Spy files

Every insider trade gets a case file. Trades that fit an unusual pattern (sold just before a drop, bought just before a jump, a large sale by a top boss, several insiders moving together) are stamped Unusual. Unusual means worth a closer look, not that anything illegal happened.

Smart calls

Everything we track, rolled into one score per stock: price, insiders, Congress, shocks and world events. Every point shows where it came from.

How the score is made

Each stock gets points from five simple rules, and every point comes with its reason. A total of +2 or more is a Buy, −2 or less is a Sell, anything in between is a Hold.

  • Price: +2 if it fell 7+ points behind the market over the past month, −2 if it ran 7+ points ahead. Short-term overreactions tend to partly reverse; in our 5-year backtest this was the only simple price rule that beat the market.
  • Insiders: +2 if two or more company insiders bought with their own money (+1 for one); −1 for heavy unplanned selling.
  • Congress: +0.5 per net buy by members of Congress, up to ±1.
  • Shocks: −1.5 for an unexplained sell-off in the last 10 days; ±0.5 depending on how it recovered from past shocks.
  • World: −0.5 if it's exposed to one of today's five biggest world events.
