Insider trades

What members of Congress and company insiders (SEC Form 4) are buying and selling.

Where insiders agree

Stocks where several people with inside knowledge are trading the same way. Buying by several insiders at once is one of the best-studied signals. Selling is weaker evidence, since insiders often sell on a schedule for taxes or diversification.

Spy files

Every insider trade gets a case file. Trades that fit an unusual pattern (sold just before a drop, bought just before a jump, a large sale by a top boss, several insiders moving together) are stamped Unusual. Unusual means worth a closer look, not that anything illegal happened.

Surprise shocks

Unusual selling, volume spikes or price drops with no clear news behind them.

We flag a stock when its price moves far more than the market explains (2.5+ times its usual swing), or when 3+ times the normal number of shares trade in a day. Then we check for a reason: news coverage that day, or an insider filing. Shocks with no reason are marked in red.

How the calls are made

Each stock gets points from simple rules, and every point comes with its reason. A total of +2 or more is a Buy, −2 or less is a Sell, anything in between is a Hold.

  • Pullback: +2 if it fell 7+ points behind the market over the past month, −2 if it ran 7+ points ahead. Short-term overreactions tend to partly reverse; in our 5-year backtest this was the only simple price rule that beat the market.
  • Insiders: +2 if two or more company insiders bought with their own money (+1 for one); −1 if three or more sold and none bought.
  • Congress: +0.5 per net buy by members of Congress, up to ±1.
  • Shocks: −1.5 for an unexplained sell-off in the last 10 days; ±0.5 depending on how it recovered from past shocks.
  • World events: −0.5 if it's exposed to one of today's five riskiest world events.
